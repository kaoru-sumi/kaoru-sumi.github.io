Phantom Sensations in Social VR

This research investigates phantom sensations experienced in immersive social virtual reality, in which users report bodily sensations despite the absence of corresponding physical stimulation.

The work examines how visual events, avatar embodiment, spatial presence, expectation, and emotional arousal contribute to perceived sensations during virtual interactions. It also explores individual differences and physiological and neural responses associated with these experiences.

Current Research Topics

  • Phantom touch and other visually induced bodily sensations
  • Avatar embodiment and body ownership
  • Spatial and social presence
  • Emotional arousal and expectation
  • Individual differences in susceptibility
  • Physiological and neural sensing
  • Experimental design for immersive social VR

Research Approach

Controlled VR scenarios are used to present visual events such as virtual contact, falling, slashing, thermal cues, and other interactions.

Subjective reports are combined with behavioral, physiological, and neural measures to investigate the mechanisms underlying phantom sensations.
